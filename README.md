# Internet Reclaimer

**We Discover What the World Has Forgotten.**  
**我们发现世界遗忘的价值。**

An open, collaborative initiative to discover, understand, assess, and responsibly revive overlooked digital value.

Internet Reclaimer invites people from every discipline to find overlooked digital assets — software, AI resources, data, digital products, research, creative works, blockchain-related resources, and things we do not yet know how to classify — then work together to understand their history, value, rights, risks, and possible second lives.

This is a community knowledge project, not a promise of financial returns and not a claim of ownership over discovered assets. Its purpose is shared discovery, careful assessment, responsible reuse, and public learning.

## Why this exists

The digital world contains decades of human creation. Some valuable work is abandoned, hidden, poorly indexed, misunderstood, or simply waiting for a new context. An asset that had little use yesterday may become useful tomorrow.

Finding something is only the beginning. We want to help answer:

- What is it, and where did it come from?
- Why was it created, and what happened to it?
- What problem could it solve today?
- What evidence supports its value?
- Who owns it, and under what conditions can it be used?
- Can it be safely and responsibly reused?
- Has anyone actually validated that reuse?

## What we are building

A shared, open method and a community-powered discovery network, supported by a free **Internet Reclaimer Skill** that helps people and AI assistants follow the same evidence-led workflow.

**Discover → Identify → Understand → Assess → Review rights and risks → Match → Validate → Reuse responsibly.**

## What counts as a digital asset?

The scope is intentionally open. Initial categories include:

1. Software and technical assets
2. AI assets: models, datasets, agents, skills, prompts, workflows
3. Blockchain and digital-finance resources
4. Mature or discontinued digital products and services
5. Research, inventions, patents, and technical knowledge
6. Data collections and digital archives
7. Creative and virtual assets
8. Unknown or emerging forms of digital value

See [the asset taxonomy](docs/ASSET-TAXONOMY.md). The `unknown` category is deliberate: our current vocabulary must not limit what the community can discover.

## How to participate

- **Discoverers:** submit a lead, even if you do not know what it is.
- **Researchers:** investigate history, provenance, and context.
- **Domain experts:** assess technical, scientific, cultural, or practical value.
- **Rights reviewers:** help clarify ownership, licenses, and restrictions.
- **Reusers:** test lawful, safe ways to put an asset to use.
- **Independent reviewers:** challenge claims and improve evidence quality.

Start with [CONTRIBUTING.md](CONTRIBUTING.md), open an Issue, or try the [Internet Reclaimer Skill](skills/internet-reclaimer/SKILL.md).

## Principles

- Evidence before claims; uncertainty is visible.
- Discovery does not confer ownership.
- Public availability does not imply permission to copy, redistribute, or commercialize.
- Preserve metadata and provenance separately from source materials and reuse rights.
- Human review for consequential judgments and actions.
- Corrections, disagreement, and takedown requests are part of a healthy project.
- Contribution quality matters more than submission volume.
- The project is community-oriented and non-profit in intent; it is not currently a registered nonprofit organization and does not promise financial returns.

## Current repository status

This repository began as **Legacy Code Atlas**. Its historical-software catalog and discovery work remain as the first vertical workstream under the broader Internet Reclaimer initiative.

The 30 files under `catalog/` are explicitly labeled planning placeholders, not verified discoveries. Do not count them as real assets.

The first Skill is an initial draft for community testing, not a deployed autonomous service or a legal/technical certification system.
