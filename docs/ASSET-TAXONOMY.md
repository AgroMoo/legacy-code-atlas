# Internet Reclaimer — Open Asset Taxonomy

This taxonomy is a starting map, not a boundary. If a discovery does not fit, use `unknown` and propose a new category.

| Category | Examples | Key assessment questions |
|---|---|---|
| software | archived repositories, libraries, tools, algorithms, frameworks | provenance, function, dependencies, maintainability, license, safe reuse |
| ai | models, weights, datasets, agents, Skills, prompts, workflows, MCP servers | origin, model/data terms, capability, evaluation, dependencies, safety |
| blockchain | public protocols, contracts, open-source tooling, project artifacts, public on-chain clues | contract provenance, security, ownership claims, legal access, protocol status |
| products | discontinued apps, SaaS, websites, digital services, product components | original users, reason discontinued, technical condition, rights, revival path |
| research | papers, prototypes, patents, technical methods, lab artifacts | authorship, evidence, reproducibility, patent status, publication terms |
| data | public datasets, maps, archives, structured collections, metadata | provenance, privacy, consent, license, quality, update history |
| creative | 3D models, game assets, digital art, audio/video, virtual-world artifacts | creator, license, cultural context, integrity, permitted transformations |
| unknown | unclassified or emerging digital resources | what is it, how can it be safely investigated, what expertise is needed? |

## Cross-cutting dimensions

Every category should be assessed using the same common dimensions, with specialist checks added where necessary:

1. **Identity:** what is the asset, and what is its canonical source?
2. **Provenance:** who created or published it, when, and through what chain?
3. **History:** why was it created, how did it evolve, and what happened?
4. **Condition:** is it available, complete, maintained, executable, readable, or damaged?
5. **Potential value:** what needs might it serve today or in the future?
6. **Evidence quality:** which claims are established, inferred, disputed, or unknown?
7. **Rights and access:** who controls it, what permissions exist, and what restrictions apply?
8. **Safety and ethics:** could investigation or reuse harm people, systems, or communities?
9. **Reuse pathway:** preserve, document, repair, adapt, combine, license, refer, or do not use.
10. **Validation:** has a real person independently confirmed a meaningful outcome?

## Value is multidimensional

Do not collapse all value into one score. A record may have:
- high historical value and low current practical value;
- high technical potential but unclear rights;
- strong public-interest value but no commercial use;
- a promising hypothesis that has not yet been tested.

Describe the dimensions and evidence separately. If a numerical score is later introduced, publish its rubric, uncertainty, and limitations.

## Rights vocabulary

Track these separately:
- preservation basis
- permission to access
- permission to copy
- permission to modify
- permission to redistribute
- permission for commercial use

Never infer one permission from another. Unknown remains UNKNOWN. This taxonomy is not legal advice.
